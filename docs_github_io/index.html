Akıllı Bölgeleme Aracı — Urban Sub-Zoning & Spatial Decision Support

Academic methodology and operational reference for street-block morphological cellularization, multi-criteria Z-score standardization, adjacency-constrained Ward minimum variance clustering, 3D vulnerability synthesis, and automated urban intervention profiling.

1. Mathematical Formulation & Algorithmic Pipeline

1.1 Street-Block Morphological Cellularization & Sliver Filtering (analiz.py :: build_cells_from_roads)

To avoid the Modifiable Areal Unit Problem (MAUP) inherent in arbitrary grids, the study boundary and road centerlines are topologically noded via QgsGeometry.unaryUnion and polygonized into street-bounded urban cells. Road medians, traffic islands, and sliver polygons lacking real building footprints are detected via Polsby-Popper compactness $PP$ and morphological thickness $T$:

$$PP(c) = \frac{4\pi A_c}{P_c^2}, \qquad T(c) = \frac{2 A_c}{P_c}$$

Cells without building blocks satisfying $A_c < 450\text{ m}^2$, $PP(c) < 0.10$, or $T(c) < 14.0\text{ m}$ (or collapsing under inward morphological erosion of $-7.0\text{ m}$) are automatically absorbed into the adjacent urban block sharing the longest boundary edge.

1.2 Winsorized Z-Score Standardization & Thematic Group Weighting (algoritma.py :: zscore_columns)

Every active indicator column $x_{i,k}$ across $n$ cells is standardized to zero mean and unit variance, clipped to $[-3.0, +3.0]$ to prevent extreme single-parcel outliers from dominating variance, and scaled by its thematic group weight $W_g \in \{W_{\text{lu}}, W_{\text{yapi}}, W_{\text{dogal}}, W_{\text{geo}}\}$:

$$z_{i,k} = \text{clip}\!\left(\frac{x_{i,k} - \mu_k}{\sigma_k},\; -3.0,\; +3.0\right), \qquad \tilde{z}_{i,k} = z_{i,k} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{W_g}{|G_g|}}$$

1.3 Adjacency-Constrained Ward Minimum Variance Clustering (algoritma.py :: ward_merge)

Following Ward (1963) (DOI: 10.1080/01621459.1963.10500845), hierarchical agglomeration is restricted strictly to spatially contiguous clusters $(A, B) \in E_{\text{adj}}$ sharing a boundary segment $> 1.0\text{ m}$. Each cell is initialized with weight $W_i = \sqrt{A_i}$, and the priority-queue merge cost incorporates an area-balance penalty ($C_{\text{area}} = \frac{\sum A_i}{K}\cdot m_{\text{cap}}$, $p=2.0$, $\varepsilon=0.05$):

$$\Delta \text{ESS}(A, B) = \frac{W_A W_B}{W_A + W_B}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{D} (\bar{z}_{A,k} - \bar{z}_{B,k})^2 + \varepsilon\right) \cdot \left(1 + \left(\frac{\text{Area}_A + \text{Area}_B}{C_{\text{area}}}\right)^2\right)$$

Upon merging cluster $B$ into $A$, the centroid feature vector updates deterministically via $\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{A \cup B} = \frac{W_A \bar{\mathbf{z}}_A + W_B \bar{\mathbf{z}}_B}{W_A + W_B}$.

1.4 Hungarian Bipartite Profile Matching (algoritma.py :: hungarian)

To assign each emergent zone $i \in \{1,\dots,K\}$ to a unique urban planning archetype $j \in \{A_1,\dots,A_{28}\}$ (or advanced intervention profiles $\{M_1,\dots,M_{28}\}$), the plugin constructs a feature-and-cardinal-direction cost matrix $C_{i,j}$ and solves the global minimum-cost bipartite assignment in $O(K^3)$ time via the Kuhn (1955) Hungarian algorithm (DOI: 10.1002/nav.3800020109).

1.5 3D Disaster Vulnerability Framework: Exposure, Sensitivity, Adaptive Capacity (analiz.py :: compute_vulnerability)

Grounded in the sustainability vulnerability framework of Turner et al. (2003) (DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1231335100), seismic and flood vulnerability indices ($V_{\text{seismic}}, V_{\text{flood}} \in [1, 5]$) combine Exposure ($E$), physical/morphological Sensitivity ($S$), and spatial/accessibility Adaptive Capacity ($AC$):

$$E = 0.6\,e_{\text{pop\_dens}} + 0.4\,e_{\text{bldg\_count}}, \qquad V_{\text{seismic}} = \frac{E \times S_{\text{seismic}}}{\max(1.0,\, AC_{\text{seismic}})}, \qquad V_{\text{flood}} = \frac{E \times S_{\text{flood}}}{\max(1.0,\, AC_{\text{flood}})}$$ $$V_{\text{total}} = 0.6\,V_{\text{seismic}} + 0.4\,V_{\text{flood}}$$

where facility accessibility components integrate Two-Step Floating Catchment Area (2SFCA) supply-to-demand principles (Luo & Wang, 2003, DOI: 10.1068/b29120) and street-network axial/segment integration concepts (Hillier et al., 1976, DOI: 10.1068/b030147).

2. The Five Methodological Invariants (Zero-Distortion Urban Rules)

Invariant 1: Industrial & Agricultural Zero-Population Rule

Implemented in cell_residential_weight and apply_population_settings. Cells and zones dominated by industrial/storage (lu_sanayi ≥ 0.45, e.g., OSB Zone A28) or agricultural/open land (lu_tarim ≥ 0.70) receive a strict population weight of $0.0$. Population is allocated exclusively to residential (lu_konut) and mixed commercial-residential ($w_{\text{konut}} + 0.35\,w_{\text{ticaret}}$) fabrics.

Invariant 2: TAKS < 5% Open-Space Order Safeguard

Implemented in zone_stats. Whenever a sub-zone exhibits building coverage $\text{TAKS} < 5\%$ or agricultural/open ratio $> 70\%$, it is never misclassified as "Bitişik Nizam" (Attached Order). Instead, its attached ratio is forced to $0.0$ and its morphological label is set to "Yapısız / Açık Alan" ($\text{TAKS} < 1.5\%$) or "Seyrek Kırsal Doku".

Invariant 3: Footprint-Weighted Floor Average

Implemented in accumulate_polygons. Mean floor count is computed as the building-footprint-weighted mean $\bar{K}_z = \frac{\sum K_i A_{\text{bldg},i}}{\sum A_{\text{bldg},i}}$, preventing isolated multi-story structures in sparse rural blocks from inflating sub-zone story averages.

Invariant 4: Explicit Heritage, Risk & Dilapidation Tracking

Implemented in get_extra_Sit_params and describe_concise. Poor building stock share (yapi:kotu_yapi %), registered heritage area share (sit2 %), and disaster-risk/renewal boundary share (sit3 %) are explicitly tracked in the Z-matrix, attribute table, and narrative summaries.

Invariant 5: Top-3 $|\Delta Z|$ Dynamic Boundary Justification

Implemented in build_contrast_explanation. Rather than static distance templates, each sub-zone's boundary separation rationale (Komsu_Bolgelerden_Ayrim_Nedeni) dynamically ranks the top 3 non-coordinate variables with the largest absolute standardized difference $|\bar{Z}_{A,k} - \bar{Z}_{B,k}|$ against adjacent zones.
